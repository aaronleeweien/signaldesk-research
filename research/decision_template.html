Potential investments: what might outperform VOO?

This section focuses on actual ETF histories and a separate exploratory stock watchlist. Outperformance is historical, not forecast. Longer holds can be measured in years, not days.

Easy interpretation: A fund that beat VOO historically is a potential long-term investment candidate. You can consider allocating future investments—but the dashboard cannot tell you an exact profitable purchase price or future selling date.

How the backend makes decisions

1. Investing (weeks / years)

Live fund history: Compare the actual performance of SPMO, XMMO, QQQ, SMH, MTUM and MDY against VOO over the same years and costs. Only past fund winners with healthy recent trends appear as stronger research candidates.

Stock watchlist: Check 30 named US companies for positive 3-/6-month momentum and trends. These are preliminary alerts, since a list of today's surviving companies biases any historical research.

2. Trading (up to 15 sessions)

Entry: The last completed closing price breaks its prior 20-session high, stays above its 200-session moving average, and has positive 63- and 126-day momentum. The simulated purchase is at the next regular US open.

Exit: Close drops at least 7% versus entry, or below its previous 10-day low, or below the 200-day average; sell the next open. Otherwise exit after a maximum of 15 sessions.

Why the evidence can still be misleading

For every ETF, the trading code simulates completed historical trades at the following open and compares a VOO position bought and sold on the exact same days. It counts trades, wins, losses, average return and excess over VOO, then estimates uncertainty with bootstrap resampling.

Even if a ticker passes the historical screen, it is not live-approved: the tickers and rules were selected after seeing older results; taxes, spreads and currency conversion are omitted; and forward brokerage-quality paper fills are still absent.

Jargon translated: Momentum = price strength over past months; SMA200 = average price over 200 market days; drawdown = worst peak-to-low fall; bootstrap = a statistical uncertainty check, not a guarantee; alpha = extra return after adjusting for a benchmark/risk, not just the stock going up.

US market hours, holidays and prices: this is a daily static GitHub update, not a live trading feed. Dates are estimated business days and do not incorporate every US exchange holiday. You must verify broker listings, fees, tax treatment and fill prices separately.
