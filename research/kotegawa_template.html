Can we profit when stocks bounce back?

My answer: Not proven yet. I would not copy Kotegawa's trades with real money.

Why? A stock falls very far → panic selling might end → the stock sometimes rises again → we might make money if we sell in time. But sometimes it keeps falling.

How the test works

25-day moving average (SMA25) = average stock closing price over the past 25 trading days.

Mean reversion = the idea that an unusually low price may bounce back.

Entry: VOO is below its 50-day average; a stock drops at least 10% below its 25-day average; pretend BUY at the next US market opening.

Exit: Pretend SELL at the opening after the stock closes back above its 25-day average, falls another 8% below purchase, or reaches five held sessions.

Trading costs: 0.2% per buy or sell, including a rough estimate for spreads and slippage. Slippage = getting a worse price than expected.
