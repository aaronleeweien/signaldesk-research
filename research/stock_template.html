Risk checks and limitations

  • Survivorship bias: This prototype looks only at a fixed list of companies known today. Delisted stocks aren't captured.
  • Other costs: The simulation omits some taxes, bid-ask spreads, foreign exchange charges and market gaps.
  • Risk exposure: A model may outperform only because it concentrates in more volatile technology stocks.
  • Data leakage: Signals use previous completed closes and simulated fills at the following opening price.
  • Future evidence: Free historical survivor data alone is insufficient for trusting live money.

Research approach — why this might work

Original momentum research found that stocks with strong performance over several months sometimes continued outperforming. Our model tests this hypothesis in a small, transparent stock universe. Jegadeesh & Titman (1993).

But price momentum can suffer sudden crashes when market conditions change. Daniel & Moskowitz documented this danger. We therefore compare a basic top-five model against trend-filtered and defensive versions rather than assuming momentum is always safe.

Sample holdings: 30 long-lived listed US stocks representing several industries, with a strict maximum 20% target allocation per stock in the main strategy. Monthly reviews; 12-to-1-month and six-month momentum; optional 200-day filter; no shorting or borrowing.

Evaluation: Separate retrospective periods for 2013–2018, 2019–2022 and 2023–present. This is not a pristine out-of-sample experiment because the stock universe was chosen knowing which companies exist today. Genuine forward monitoring must start from signals recorded now.

Data: Yahoo Finance via yfinance, adjusted price histories. A failure to download data blocks new reports. Source and full metrics are saved under SignalDesk GitHub. No live trading, no account connection, no paid data or analytics subscription.
